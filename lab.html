MacBook station · not a medical device

Live listen for the missing band.

Sit the laptop by your sister. Put headphones on him. The Mac hears her f and s and copies them down into a band he may still have.

Mac setup

  1. Plug in wired over-ears if you can. Bluetooth ANC often lags 150–300 ms and will feel like a bad phone call. Many ANC sets still take a cable.
  2. System Settings → Sound: Output = his headphones. Input = MacBook Microphone (or a cheap lav on your sister).
  3. Turn Mac volume to about half. He wears the headphones before you press start, or the laptop speakers will howl.
  4. Point the top of the lid toward your sister — that is the mic. Aids out if you can, so you are not stacking two processors.
  5. The audiogram starts on Ski-slope highs (the usual f/s pattern). Drag it to match his test if you have one.

He must hear aid output — the remapped sound — not the “his ear” demo path. Live mode locks that for you.

3. The other algorithm: captions

When is it caption territory?

  1. The landing band is gone. Frequency lowering only helps if some hearing remains around 1–2 kHz to receive the moved sound. If 500, 1 kHz and 2 kHz are all severe or worse, there is nowhere useful to put the missing band.
  2. He hears talking, not words. That sentence — “I know you’re speaking, I don’t know what you said” — is the clinical hint. Loudness is not the problem. The speech code is.
  3. Television, phone, and the dinner table already fail with the aids he has. Put captions on those now. Do not wait for a perfect aid. Lowering can still be tried; captions do not have to wait for the trial.
  4. Severe–profound in both ears, or aided word scores that stay poor in quiet, is cochlear-implant assessment territory as well as caption territory.

Captions are not a consolation prize. They are the reliable algorithm. Lowering is a maybe that needs an audiologist and weeks of wear-in. Use both.
